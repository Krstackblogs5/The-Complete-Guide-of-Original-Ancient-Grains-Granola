What Are Ancient Grains?

Ancient grains are grains that have remained largely unchanged for hundreds or even thousands of years. Unlike refined modern grains, these varieties maintain their original structure, flavor, and nutrient composition. Examples of ancient grains commonly used in granola blends include quinoa, millet, amaranth, sorghum, and other heritage grains. These grains are valued not only for their history but for how they perform in modern recipes.

Why Ancient Grains Matter in Granola

Ancient grains bring qualities that refined grains often lack:

  • Greater texture diversity, creating a more engaging eating experience
  • Naturally complex flavors, often nutty or earthy
  • Broader nutrient profiles, depending on the grain

When used in granola, ancient grains create layers of crunch and chew that feel more substantial than uniform oat-based blends. This complexity reduces the need for excessive sweeteners or flavoring. Ancient granola appeals to buyers who want more than convenience; it appeals to those who value thoughtful ingredient selection and craftsmanship.
